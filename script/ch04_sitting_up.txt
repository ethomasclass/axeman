Then August. And things speed up.

August 5th. Anna Schneider, eight months pregnant, wakes up to a dark figure standing over her bed. She's beaten badly. She survives, and two days later, she has a healthy baby girl.

August 10th. Joseph Romano, an elderly man living with his two nieces, is struck in the head. The nieces see a man running away. Heavy-set. Dark suit. Slouch hat. Romano dies two days later.

In the yard, police find a bloody axe. And on the back door, a panel chiseled away.

Now the newspapers have a name for him. *The Axeman.*

And New Orleans panics. People report a prowler on every block. Men sit up all night with shotguns across their knees. A few call the police to report that they've found an axe in their backyard. Which, in 1918, is where a lot of people kept their axe.

That's mass hysteria. *Fear spreading faster than the facts.*

And then, nothing. That fall, the flu shuts down the city's schools, churches and theaters. In November, the war ends. And for seven months, the Axeman is quiet.
