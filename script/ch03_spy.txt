Five weeks later. June 27th. Another grocery, another back room.

Louis Besumer, a grocer, and Harriet Lowe, the woman living with him, are attacked with a hatchet in the night. A bakery driver finds them the next morning. Alive. Barely.

Then things get strange. It's 1918. America is at war with Germany, and the whole country is jumpy about spies. Police find letters in Besumer's home written in foreign languages. And Harriet Lowe tells them he's a German spy.

He isn't. They hold him for two days and let him go.

But in August, Lowe goes in for surgery on her injuries, and she doesn't recover. Before she dies, she names her attacker. Louis Besumer.

So the man who was attacked is now charged with murder. He spends nine months in jail. At his trial in May 1919, the jury takes about ten minutes to find him not guilty.

Ten minutes. That's barely enough time to find your coat.

Remember Besumer. *He won't be the last person blamed for this.*
