May 23rd, 1918. Uptown New Orleans, the corner of Upperline and Magnolia.

Joseph Maggio runs a grocery on the ground floor. He and his wife Catherine live in the back. Like a lot of corner grocers in the city, they're Italian immigrants.

Sometime before dawn, someone chisels a panel out of their back door. Just big enough to crawl through. Inside, the intruder finds an axe that belongs to the Maggios.

Joseph's brothers live next door. They find the couple a few hours later. Catherine is already dead. Joseph dies minutes later.

Here's what bothers the police. Money and jewelry are sitting right there, in plain sight. *Nobody took them.* So this wasn't a robbery.

The razor used in the attack belongs to Joseph's brother Andrew, a barber. Andrew is arrested. Then released. He says he's innocent, and nobody can prove otherwise.

And a block away, someone has written a message in chalk on the sidewalk: "Mrs. Maggio will sit up tonight, just like Mrs. Toney."

Police guessed that meant the wife of Anthony Schiambra, another Italian grocer, attacked back in 1912. Newspapers started digging up old cases from 1911, too. Were they connected? Nobody could prove that either.

Hold onto that back door. You're going to see it again.
