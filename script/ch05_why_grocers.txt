So why corner grocers? And why so many Italian families?

By 1918, New Orleans has one of the biggest Italian communities in the South, most of them from Sicily. A lot of them run small groceries on corners all over the city, with the family living in the back.

To a lot of newspapers, that pointed one way. The Mafia. Or the Black Hand: gangs that sent letters demanding money, and threatened to kill you if you didn't pay. That really did happen in New Orleans.

But there's a problem. The Black Hand wanted money. *The Axeman never took any.*

And there's a darker history behind how fast people reached for "Mafia." In 1891, after the city's police chief was murdered, a mob broke into the New Orleans jail and killed 11 Italian men. Some of them had just been found not guilty. It's often called the largest mass lynching in American history.

So when the Axeman hit Italian homes, plenty of New Orleanians saw the Mafia. A lot of Italian families saw something else. *They were the ones being hunted.* And nobody seemed to be protecting them.

Same evidence. Two very different stories.
