Play jazz, or get the axe.

In March 1919, a letter signed by a serial killer made that offer to New Orleans.

And the city was terrified. Since May 1918, someone had been chiseling through back doors at night and attacking families in their beds, usually with an axe.

A grocer and his wife. A woman who died weeks later. An elderly man. And just three days before the letter, a little girl, killed in her mother's arms.

Then the letter ran in the Times-Picayune. It was dated from Hell. And it was signed: the Axeman.

At 12:15 on Tuesday night, he would pass over the city. Every home with a jazz band in full swing would be spared. Anyone who didn't jazz it out would get the axe.

So New Orleans jazzed it out. Dance halls packed. Bands at house parties all over town.

12:15 came and went.

*Nobody was attacked that night.*

The letter was almost certainly a prank. And the Axeman? *He was never caught.*
