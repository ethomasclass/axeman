So who was he?

A couple of years later, newspapers reported that in Los Angeles, Mike Pepitone's widow had shot and killed a man. She said he was the man who murdered her husband.

It's a perfect ending. It's also hard to check. The records that would prove the man she shot was the Axeman have never turned up.

There are other suspects. A man with a long criminal record. A killer who was really after the women in each house. The Mafia. Copycats. Or several different attackers, stitched into one monster by the newspapers. Some of the attacks don't match the others at all.

So let's go back to the question. *How did a killer get a whole city to throw him a party?*

He probably didn't. The attacks were real, and so was the fear. A city where men sat up all night with shotguns. Newspapers that gave the fear a name. Innocent people blamed, again and again.

And then a letter, almost certainly fake, that finally gave people something to do with all that fear. Something they were good at.

New Orleans never learned the Axeman's name. So it answered him the way New Orleans answers almost everything.

*It played.*
